How this works, and why it isn't just this season's table

Each match is two Poisson processes: home goals ~ Poisson(λ), away goals ~ Poisson(μ), where λ = αhome·βaway·γ and μ = αaway·βhome. α is attack strength, β defensive weakness, γ home advantage. Dixon & Coles (1997) add a correction τ with parameter ρ, because independent Poisson underrates 0-0 and 1-1. Everything is fit by maximum likelihood.

The early-season problem. With 20 teams there are 42 parameters. Three matchweeks is about 30 matches, so an unconstrained fit blows up — a team that hasn't conceded gets an infinitely good defense, and a team that hasn't scored gets an attack of zero. This model handles that two ways: last season's matches are included with exponential time-decay (the "last season weight" slider is the half-life in days), and a ridge penalty shrinks every team toward the league average (the "shrinkage" slider). Turn shrinkage to 0 early in the season and you can watch the fit break.

Promoted teams have no top-flight history, so they start from a prior measured from how the previous promoted cohort actually performed, then move as results arrive.
